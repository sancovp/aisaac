# Explainer script shape (the Tom Noske shape)

Why his scripts feel clean: everything is explained, nothing is left over, and the video itself is
an example of what it teaches. These are the moves, in order.

## The moves

1. **PROMISE in one sentence**: what the viewer will be able to do by the end ("sales every day").
   Write it on screen and come back to it.
2. **ONE PHYSICAL METAPHOR that splits the category** before any point is made. His: the bike gear
   (considered purchase) versus the chamois cream (impulse purchase that removes a pain). The viewer
   understands the whole distinction before hearing a single rule. It is a thing from his own life,
   in a photo.
3. **EACH POINT AS A TWO-ENDED SCALE WITH A CONSEQUENCE AT EACH END**: "low X → bad outcome, high
   X → good outcome." Same pattern every time, so the viewer learns it once and each new point costs
   them nothing.
4. **EACH POINT SHOWN WITH AN OBJECT HE OWNS**: the paper to-do card, the $9 sheet he still uses, the
   LUTs on his camera right now. A real thing that is visibly used, never a hypothetical.
5. **A WORKED GRADING**: run two real examples through every point, out loud. The viewer learns to
   grade by watching it done.
6. **THE HARD TRUTH, SAID KINDLY**: the point that will disqualify some viewers' plans ("hard-to-explain
   workflows can't be a digital product"), followed by what those things ARE still good for.
7. **THE FINISH-LINE IMAGE, LAST AND DEEPEST**: a second photo, an emotional story (the finish-line
   photo, the wife he didn't see), and the reframe it carries: people pay for the moment, not the
   gear. Then the loop that sells the product, drawn.
8. **THE VIDEO IS ITS OWN DEMO**: point at the fact that you are doing the thing right now ("I'm
   making content about it right now"; LUTs off, on). This is the "so meta" quality.
9. **NEXT-VIDEO CLOSE**: the natural next problem, and the video that solves it. The loop runs on
   the viewer.

## The look (cheap to make)

He sits on screen with a note-taking app that tidies handwriting into slightly hand-drawn lines.
He draws small diagrams and scales as he talks, and now and then shows a photo. Nothing else.
Our closest tool is the `excalidraw-render` skill (Excalidraw's sketchy hand-drawn style), drawn
around *USER*'s own footage the way the streamer fork already composites (`video-aios/DESIGN.md` §A.-1.2).

## Check a draft against it

For each move, quote the line of the draft that does it, or write MISSING. A draft with a MISSING
move 2, 4 or 8 is not finished: those three are what make it feel clean.

CoR: "promise {P}; metaphor {M}; points {scale…}; objects {…}; grading {A, B}; finish line {F}; self-demo {D}; next {N}."
