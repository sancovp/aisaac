# Teach, and sell only incidentally (the Tom Noske mechanism)

The viewer is never sold to. They are taught an INSTRUMENT, they watch it graded on examples, and the
examples are what is for sale. Then they run the instrument on themselves and find their own gap.
By the time an offer appears the decision is already made, and the offer is free, so taking it costs
nothing. Selling is a side effect of the teaching being TRUE ABOUT ITSELF.

## THE CORE: the piece is a fixpoint of what it teaches

The video teaches "content that shows a product sells that product every day", and it IS content
that shows products. Every level of it is an instance of the claim made at that level, so nothing in
it is unexplained and nothing is left over. That is what "everything fits" means here: F(piece) = piece.

## THE ROLL-UP — each level, with an example that is itself an instance of that level

| level | what Tom does | what makes it an instance |
|---|---|---|
| OVERVIEW: the promise | "sales every day" is said 13 times in ~3,300 words | the promise is the refrain, so every part is heard as serving it |
| OVERVIEW: the metaphor | bike gear (considered) vs chamois cream (impulse, removes a pain) | a real thing from his life that splits the category before any rule is given |
| PARTS: the instrument | five tests, each scored 1–10 | an instrument the viewer can pick up and use, not a list of tips |
| ATTRIBUTES: each test | the same sentence every time: question → scale → "if you're over here… you have X. Whereas over here… you'll have Y" | identical grammar means each new test costs the viewer nothing; the form is a generator |
| ATTRIBUTES: each test's object | the to-do card · a $9 sheet he has filled in monthly since 2019 · his LUTs on the camera filming this video | owned, used and visible, never hypothetical |
| COMPOSE: the grading | the cream AND his own LUTs scored on all five, out loud | **the sale, inside a worked example**: the viewer scores his product 5/5 with him, and the "off / on" demo shows it working. He never pitches it |
| COMPOSE: the live proof | he recommends the $9 sheet to an audience that didn't exist when he bought it | the claim "a daily product gets talked about" HAPPENS on screen: he is the word of mouth he describes |
| COMPOSE: the handover | "rank your own product against these five" | the viewer runs the instrument on their own work and discovers their own gap |
| COMPOSE: the loop | content → problem → purchase → finish line → content | he places the viewer inside it ("you are watching this because of a problem"): the viewer is its current instance |
| CLOSE: his own finish line | the Ironman finisher photo, the $20,000 "for the photo", his wife at the rail | the deepest teaching (people buy the moment) told as HIS before/after, which is the content step of his own loop |
| CLOSE: the offer | "watch this one next" | the loop's purchase step at price zero. The decision was made during the video, the offer costs nothing, so the viewer acts, and the next video starts the loop again |

## THE THREE LEVELS (DONGSUM): product · content · identity

Every piece explains three levels and gives each one an example that is an instance of it:
**OUTER = the product** (what is sold) · **INNER = the content** (how the viewer comes to want it) ·
**SECRET = the identity** (what anyone really buys: the name they get at the finish line — "you are an
Iron Man"). Each level opens into its own outer/inner/secret (a 3×3 grid). The cell that does the
selling is SECRET-of-SECRET: the viewer leaves already treating themselves as the person the product
names, before any offer. The full grid for Tom's video, its 13-station map, its 3⁴ decomposition and
its AC^AC reading: `docs/tom-noske-mechanism.md`. Build a new piece by filling that grid in first.

## THE VERBIAGE AND GRAMMAR

- **Crude, sticky handles for concepts**: "ass pain medication", "content compatibility", "the content to
  sales loop". A named concept is a handle the viewer keeps. Repeat each name (content compatibility
  and "proven" are each said ten times).
- **One sentence template per point**, kept identical across all the points (above).
- **Second person, and present tense about the viewer's own situation**: "your product", "you're
  watching this because…".
- **Hard truths with a concession**: "I know that's a hard one to hear… it doesn't mean X, it just
  means Y". This disqualifies a plan without losing the person.
- **Spoken, plain, a little rough**: no polish, so it reads as one person who does this, talking.
- **A self-demonstration said out loud**: "I'm making content about it right now". The instance is
  pointed at, so the viewer sees the fixpoint.

## THE ORDER (never change it)

1. Promise. 2. The metaphor that splits the category. 3. The instrument, one test at a time, each with
an owned object. 4. The grading of examples, OUR product among them, never singled out. 5. The hard
truth with its concession. 6. The handover: the viewer grades their own case. 7. The loop drawn, with
the viewer inside it. 8. Our own finish line as the last story. 9. A free offer that continues the loop.

The PRICE never appears in the teaching. Offers are *USER*'s alone (`.claude/rules/00-SITE-CANON.md`
HARD LAW 1). This skill places an existing offer as an example; it never invents one.

## THE GATE — a draft passes only if every line is answered with the draft's own words

1. What does this piece teach, and how is the piece itself an instance of it?
2. What is the instrument the viewer leaves holding?
3. Which example is ours, and where is it graded with the same instrument as the others?
4. What claim HAPPENS on screen instead of being stated?
5. Where does the viewer run the instrument on their own case?
6. Where is the viewer placed inside the loop?
7. What is our own finish-line story?
8. Is the offer free and the next step of the same loop?
9. Do the points share one sentence template, and does each concept have a handle repeated at least three times?
10. Are all nine DONGSUM cells (product · content · identity, each outer/inner/secret) filled with a real move from the draft?
11. What name does the viewer get at the finish line, and where does the draft give it to them?
12. Is every later section a closure over what came before (a `+` in 3⁴ notation), never a new peer topic?

Any line that has to be answered "nowhere" means the piece sells instead of teaches. Rewrite that part.

CoR: "this teaches {I}; it is an instance because {F}; our example {E} is graded at {t}; the viewer runs it at {h}; the free next step is {N}."
