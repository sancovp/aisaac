# The creator invariants — what the studied creators share (the spine of the cross-creator metasystem)

One table, one column per creator studied. A row is a candidate invariant; a cell is where that creator
keeps it, or `—`. **What every column keeps is an invariant; what only one keeps belongs to that creator's
format or model.** The fourth layer — the compiler — is what the shared rows imply together
(`docs/tom-noske-mechanism.md` §2: at three stacks the triadic generator shows). Grades per
`correspondence-grading`; each cell's evidence is in its study.

Studies: [Tom Noske](tom-noske-mechanism.md) (a teaching video) · [Maria Wendt](maria-wendt-mechanism.md) (an
interview + the channel around it) · [Peter Steinberger](peter-steinberger-mechanism.md) (a long interview).

**THE COMPILER, from the shared rows (G6 — a reading of three stacks):** the maker hands the audience the thing
itself (3), names who they become when it works (5), keeps the next step free and one move away (6, 7) — and
the thing is strongest when it is shown working in front of people (1, 9), made by someone whose earlier run
already taught them the game (10). Rows 2 and 8 are FORMAT (a teacher's instrument, a seller's ledger), kept as
options a piece may add, never required.

**FOR THIS BUSINESS:** the case study is JobWorld working, used where others can see it (rows 1, 9); the free
next step is the framework or the agent itself (6, 7); the name at the finish line is still *USER*'s to write
(5 — `docs/tom-noske-mechanism.md` §5).

A new study adds a column and re-reads every row; a row that drops to one column moves under "format".
