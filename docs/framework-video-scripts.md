# The framework page — the section videos (scripts to record)

**What this is:** one short video of Isaac per section of `framework.html`. *USER*'s structure: the
page is a **funnel of funnels** — every section runs the same four beats, **these pains → this problem →
this solution → this unique way**, then hands the viewer to the next section. Each video plays when its
section scrolls into view, the scroll holds on the section while it plays, and the section's text
stays below it as the reference for skimmers.

**How to record (the streamer format — `video-aios/DESIGN.md` §A.-1.2):** sit in your desk chair,
camera at eye level, landscape, framed from mid-chest up with room above your head and to both
sides. The background is removed in the edit, so behind you: a plain wall, nothing moving, no window.
Light from the front, not behind. Wear a solid colour that is not the wall's colour. Use the best
microphone you have — your voice is the whole soundtrack. One take per script; if a line goes wrong,
pause and say it again — the edit cuts on your words. Read each line as written: one line per
breath, no line relies on its punctuation.

**Sources:** the method (`docs/the-transformation-method-2026-09-17.md` §1, §1a) · the autonomy
framework (avi-jw `AUTONOMY-MANIFESTO.md` §II.2–II.4) · the bootcamp (`b2b-bootcamp/`) · ChainCompiler
(`README.md`, `DIETC-AIOS-REBUILD.md`, `skills/skill2framework/`) · the paper (*Flat versus Tree*).
No prices, no tiers, no guarantees anywhere — the offer is Isaac's.

## 2 · THE AUTONOMY LADDER (≈75 s)

**Pains**
You hand something to AI and it goes wrong.
So you take it all back and you do it yourself again.
Now you trust AI even less than before.

**Problem**
The problem wasn't the AI.
You handed the whole thing over at once and skipped the part where it earns your trust.

**Solution**
So hand it over in grades.
At grade one you approve every piece of work before it runs.
At grade two it runs on its own and you check it afterwards.
At grade three it runs as independently as you decided it should.

**Unique way**
There are three roles in this.
The workers do the work.
An AI manager watches the work and tunes how it's set up.
And you set the goals and say yes or no which means you govern it instead of managing it.
And trust never carries over so anything new starts back at grade one.

## 3 · THE B2B BOOTCAMP — for operators (≈90 s)

**Pains**
If you sell AI to businesses you already know this.
Every prospect has heard AI transformation a hundred times.
Your calls turn into feature comparisons and price objections and maybe I'll think about it.

**Problem**
You're selling when you should be diagnosing.

**Solution**
So run it like a clinic.
First a broadcast that names a condition they didn't know they had.
Then a five minute screening where you ask how much AI does your business have and they say zero.
Then the diagnosis where they see the leak for themselves and the treatment you prescribe.
And then you keep caring for them after the sale.

**Unique way**
When they see the leak themselves you don't have to close them.
Saying yes feels like being treated instead of being sold.
And because the care keeps going you get the reviews and the referrals and the next project.

## 4 · BUILD AN AIOS (≈75 s)

**Pains**
Your AI agent forgets things.
It does the job differently every time and you keep rewriting the same prompts.

**Problem**
The way the work should be done lives in your head and in old chats.
It isn't written down anywhere the agent actually reads.

**Solution**
So you build an AIOS.
It's a folder an AI reads into and becomes.
It holds who the agent is here and the rules it runs by and a tree of skills for how to think and decide and what it has learned so far.

**Unique way**
Only the things that have to actually run are code.
The math and the files and the APIs.
Everything else is an instruction the agent reads.
So any agent you point at the folder can run your operating system.

## 5 · THE LEVELS OF CHAINCOMPILER (≈75 s)

**Pains**
You've got a pile of prompts and skills and tools and it keeps growing.
And the more you give your agent the worse it gets at picking the right one.

**Problem**
That's not the model being dumb.
A flat pile has no structure so the agent has to rebuild it from scratch on every single task.

**Solution**
ChainCompiler gives it the structure.
A gate that stops the model from drifting out of its own notation.
Chains for how to think and chains for how to decide and chains that string skills together.
All of it organized as a tree the agent walks one layer at a time.

**Unique way**
Everything is one kind of thing which is a skill folder.
That's why the levels stack.
At the top is an agent that decides every task whether to reuse a chain that already worked or build a new one and it keeps whatever works.

## 6 · TOME — publish what you built (≈60 s)

**Pains**
You build something good and then it just sits in a folder.
Nobody sees it and six months later even you forget how it works.

**Problem**
Building the thing and telling people about it feel like two separate jobs so the second one never happens.

**Solution**
So publishing becomes six steps you run on what you already built.
The story and the mechanics and the chapter and a front door skill and a plugin.
And then it gets folded into your TOME.

**Unique way**
The story is filled in from the build's own history so you're not writing from scratch.
And your TOME is one searchable place that holds every framework you've ever made and sends you straight to the right one.
