## 2. A FRANCHISOR IS NOT A CLIENT — IT IS A DISTRIBUTION CHANNEL

The export already says this and it is the most valuable sentence in the whole corpus:

> "Ribcage → model owner → operators" … **"The customer can become the distribution
> layer for the transformed system."**

⇒ **One franchisor sale is access to 10–100 pre-qualified operators** who already pay
that franchisor for the operating model, already adopt what the franchisor endorses, and
are contractually disposed to run what the system tells them to run. No other client type
available to Isaac carries that.

So the engagement has **three revenue shapes, not one**, and only the first is a retainer:

| phase | what it is | shape |
|---|---|---|
| **1 — the model** | map and augment the operating model itself | **$10–12K/mo embedded** — same rhythm as Alex, pointed at `M` instead of one business |
| **2 — the pilot** | prove it in 1–3 units | scoped project, five figures |
| **3 — the rollout** | the transformed model reaches every unit | **per-unit fee, licence, or access to sell to the units** — this is the large one, and it is negotiated only after phase 2 produces evidence |

⛔ **Do not price phase 3 at the start and do not promise network-wide transformation on
the first call.** The export is explicit: `MODEL → OPPORTUNITY MAP → PILOT → EVIDENCE →
SYSTEM UPDATE → ROLLOUT`, and *"the main risk is central decision without local
adoption."* Phase 3 is the prize; phase 2 is what earns the right to name a number for it.

## 3. ⭐ THE ENTRY ASSET — build their opportunity map from public documents, before the call

**This is what makes the play work without a case study in the segment, and it is the
reason the earlier sequencing advice was wrong.**

Every franchisor that sells franchises in the US files a **Franchise Disclosure
Document**, and registration states publish them. An FDD is not marketing — it is a
regulator-compelled description of the operating model. Roughly:

| item | what it hands you |
|---|---|
| **Item 1** | the business and the system, in the franchisor's own words |
| **Item 11** | **training, operations assistance, the manual, the required systems and software** — the operating model's own inventory |
| **Item 19** | financial performance representations — **unit economics**, where they are given |
| **Item 20** | unit counts, openings, closures, transfers, **and a list of current franchisees with contact details** |
| **Item 21** | the franchisor's own financials |

⇒ **You can compute the value of a model improvement per unit, times the unit count,
before you ever speak to them** — and then open with it. *"Playing the music"* is the
entire sales mechanism (*"Yes. That is my business."*), and at this tier **the music is
already written down and filed with a state regulator.**

⇒ And **Item 20 gives you the operators too.** One FDD is the franchisor *and* the
network, which means every franchisor you research is also a list of Channel A prospects
even if the franchisor says no.

⚠ Availability, currency and per-state rules vary and should be verified before any
outreach spend — but the asymmetry stands: **no other buyer tier publishes its own
operating model and its own customer list as a condition of doing business.**

## 4. WHAT A FRANCHISOR ACTUALLY BUYS — and it is not cost savings

A franchisor's economics are royalty on unit revenue plus unit sales. So the pitch is not
"save money." It is:

> **A more profitable, more consistent unit is a more sellable franchise.** Improve the
> model and you improve every unit's economics, every operator's satisfaction, renewal
> rates — and the number you can put in Item 19 when you sell the next franchise.

That last clause is the one that lands: **Item 19 is the franchisor's own sales
document**, and this work is how the numbers in it get better. The pitch and the
deliverable close the same loop.

The message concept is already written in the export and does not need improving:

> *"You already built the operating system. Your operators use it to reproduce the
> business. AI changes what that operating system can do. We map the model, identify
> where AI should augment it, and pilot the highest-value changes before network
> rollout."*

## 5. WHO, SPECIFICALLY — 10 to 100 units

The export's band, and its reasons hold exactly:

- the founder/CEO or COO is still reachable — **one decision-maker, like Alex**
- the operating model exists but is still actively changing
- repetition already creates leverage
- **procurement does not yet look like an enterprise**
- a five-figure initial engagement is meaningful to them rather than rounding error

**Where they are findable:** FDD registries in the registration states; franchise
directories and the Entrepreneur/Franchise Times listings; the IFA and its supplier
membership; franchise development conferences; franchise brokers and consultants, who
know every emerging brand personally and are a warm-intro layer nobody uses for this.

**Bias the list toward information-heavy systems** — home services back-office, clinics,
tutoring, staffing, property management, food with heavy ordering and scheduling —
and away from the atoms problem that capped Alex's niche
([niche-selection §1](niche-selection-2026-09-16.md)).

## 6. WHY THIS DOES NOT REPLACE THE AGENCY TRACK

Run both. They cost different things and they fail differently.

| | franchisor | agencies |
|---|---|---|
| cycle | slower, more stakeholders | fast, one owner — **Alex's proven shape** |
| payoff | enormous; a channel, not a client | reliable; $8K each |
| risk | may take a quarter to land one | may never compound |
| role | **the prize** | **the hedge, and the cash that pays for waiting** |

⚠ **Concentration gets worse, not better, in this plan.** Alex plus one franchisor is
$216K/yr from **two** month-to-month relationships either party can end with written
notice. Golden and fragile are the same arithmetic. One or two agencies at $8K are what
stop a single bad conversation from taking income to zero — and at ~3 delivery hours each
([the $100K plan §7a](the-100k-plan-2026-09-16.md)) they are nearly free to carry.
