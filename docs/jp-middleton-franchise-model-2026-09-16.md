## 6a. The title is the proof

I read *"one client I signed in 2019 sent me 200 more"* as **200 more clients to serve**,
and built §3 on it: that Door B means running 200 engagements, needing a delivery org.

**The video is called "This AI Business Only Needs One Client."** If the 2019 client

would be false. It only parses one way:

> **The ONE client is the licensor. They are the entire revenue relationship. The 200 are
> deployments underneath a license, not accounts you sold.**

And it reconciles the loose end §1 left hanging — *2,000+ businesses served* against *one
client*. Those are **users under licenses, not customers acquired.** The licensor buys,
the licensor distributes, the units consume. One invoice.

## 6b. "Franchise" is the instance. **LICENSE is the predicate.**

The second half of the correction is the more valuable one, and **the export already had
it** — `04_franchisor_strategy`, first line: *"The interesting class is broader than the
legal term 'franchisor.'"* That sentence was sitting there and neither of us used it.

The qualifying predicate is not franchising. It is:

> **an entity that licenses an operating model to many instances, AND can push a new
> component INTO that model.**

Which opens the target class far past franchising:

| class | why it qualifies |
|---|---|
| franchisors | the canonical case; can mandate systems |
| **SaaS vendors with an installed base** | **the purest version — they already license software to N businesses; an AI layer is a new module** |
| dealer & distributor networks | the manufacturer sets the operating standard |
| buying groups, co-ops, insurance clusters, agency networks | member-wide tooling is the reason they exist |
| PE platforms / MSOs | mandate is total; standardisation is the thesis |
| coaching & consulting communities with a playbook | the playbook *is* the licensed model, and they move fast |
| master franchisees, area developers | the same predicate one level down |

## 6c. ⭐ THE QUALIFYING QUESTION

Not *"how many units?"* but:

> **Can they make their operators adopt it — or can they only recommend it?**

A franchisor that already mandates a POS and a required vendor list can mandate this. An
association that merely endorses cannot. **That one question separates a $240K
relationship from a referral list**, and it is askable on the first call.

## 6e. It collapses Door A into Door B

§3 framed them as two doors requiring two different products. Under the license reading

```text
map the licensed MODEL              ← Door A, $10-12K/mo, sellable now
  → the map names the ONE JOB every operator in that network needs
    → build that job as a COMPONENT of the model     ← jobworld delivers it

```

**JP needed seven years and a lucky 2019 client to discover which single job every gym

## 6f. ⛔ TWO WARNINGS THIS MODEL CREATES

1. **Keep the IP.** A licensor will ask for work-for-hire or exclusivity. **License it to
   them; do not sell it.** The moment the component is theirs outright, the second
   licensor — the whole point of the model — becomes unsellable. Exclusivity, if granted
   at all, is narrow, time-boxed and paid for.
2. **Concentration goes from bad to extreme.** One licensor can be 80%+ of revenue and can
   also replace you by rebuilding the component once they see it work. Mitigations: the
   component depends on something they do not own; per-unit billing rather than a flat
   buyout; and a second licensor in a non-competing vertical as early as possible.
