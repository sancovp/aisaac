## 1. SCORE ALEX'S NICHE FIRST — you cannot say "better" without it

Luxury residential AV / home-theater integration, on Hormozi's four (`hormozi-market-selection`)
plus four that are specific to *this* offer and come straight out of the AHT notes:

| criterion | Alex's niche | why |
|---|---|---|
| **Pain** | 🟢 high | real, urgent, and he paid for it inside 18 hours |
| **Purchasing power** | 🟢 good | luxury margins, owner controls the cheque |
| **Easy to target** | 🟡 medium | an association exists, but the universe of integrators big enough to afford this is small |
| **Growing** | 🟡 medium | fine, not a tailwind |
| **Information density** | 🔴 **LOW** | **trucks, installers, physical product — a large share of the work cannot be automated at any price** |
| **Tacit load** | 🟢 high | "the business appears to contain many tacit processes" — the fuel for mapping |
| **Software sprawl** | 🟢 high | accumulated SaaS solving local problems |
| **Replication factor** | 🔴 **1** | **no model owner above him; every integrator is idiosyncratic, so closing one teaches you little about the next** |
| **Documentation pressure** | 🔴 low | nothing forces him to write processes down |

⇒ **Alex's niche is excellent on pain and tacit load and bad on exactly two things:
information density and replication.** Those two are the whole of "better," and they are
the two that decide whether this becomes a business or stays a sequence of one-offs.

**And one finding from the AHT notes is the reason any of this is portable:** *"the
engagement is useful precisely because the transformation value did not depend on Isaac
being a home-theater-domain specialist."* **Domain expertise is not the constraint.** So
the niche can be chosen on structure alone — which is exactly what makes choosing one
worth doing.

## 2. THE TWO UPGRADES, AND WHY THEY ARE THE TWO

### Upgrade 1 — INFORMATION DENSITY: pick businesses that move information, not atoms

An AV integrator's day is trucks, installs and physical product. **The automatable
fraction is capped no matter how good the map is.** A title company, an insurance agency
or a billing operation is *entirely* information — intake, documents, checks, approvals,
handoffs. Every single step is mappable and a large share is automatable.

⇒ **Same engagement, same hours, two or three times the addressable surface.** This is
the single biggest upgrade available and it costs nothing to take.

### Upgrade 2 — REPLICATION: pick businesses that sit under a model owner

Straight out of the export (`04_franchisor_strategy`):

```text
OWNER OF MODEL → MODEL → {Business_1 … Business_n}
value of a model improvement ≈ value per instance × number of instances
```

Alex is n = 1. **A niche where a model owner sits above the operators is the same work
priced against a multiple instead of a single P&L.**

## 3. THE SCORECARD — use this on any niche, not just the ones below

Score 0–2 each. **Anything under 12, walk. Under 8, do not take the call.**

| # | criterion | the question |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Pain | can you say their pain back to them in one sentence they'd recognise? |
| 2 | Purchasing power | can they sign $8K/mo without a board? |
| 3 | Easy to target | is there a **published list** of them? |
| 4 | Growing | tailwind or headwind? |
| 5 | **Information density** | **what fraction of the work is information rather than atoms?** |
| 6 | Tacit load | how much lives only in someone's head? |
| 7 | Software sprawl | how many systems that don't talk? |
| 8 | **Replication** | **is there a model owner above them, or are they one of many like-instances?** |
| 9 | Documentation pressure | does regulation already force them to write process down? |
| 10 | Owner reachability | is the decision-maker one person, and findable? |

**Criterion 9 is the quiet one.** A regulated business has already been forced to write
half the map, has budget lines for compliance work, and has a deadline doing the selling
for you. Alex scores zero there; the best niches score two.

## 4. THE RANKING

### 🥇 FOR THE NEXT TWO CLIENTS — one information-only, owner-operated vertical

Close like Alex closed: one owner, one conversation, fast. **The motion is already
proven; only the vertical changes.** Candidates, all pure-information:

| vertical | notes | score |
|---|---|---|
| **independent insurance agencies** | ~36,000 in the US; famous software sprawl (agency management system + a dozen carrier portals); commission revenue is predictable so $8K is affordable; owner-operated; dense associations and agency clusters; **being rolled up hard right now, which is the bridge to the tier below** | **~18** |
| title & escrow | pure information, enormous document load, regulated | ~16, but rate-sensitive |
| mid-size accounting / bookkeeping | max information density, consolidating fast | ~16, brutal seasonality |
| specialty medical & dental groups (3–30 sites) | high documentation pressure, replication already present | ~17, slower cycle |

**Recommendation: independent insurance agencies.** Alone among these it scores well on
*every* criterion including replication — agency networks and clusters are model owners,
and the PE rollup wave means a good agency client is a direct introduction to the tier
below. It is also the closest match to Alex's *decision shape* (one owner, fast yes), so
the sales motion transfers without being relearned.

⛔ **And then commit.** `hormozi-market-selection`: *"niches earn the niches."* Two or
three engagements in **one** vertical is what produces comparable traces, and comparable
traces are what the export says turns one witness into an invariant. Two clients in two
different verticals produces neither.

### 🥈 THE DESTINATION — PE-backed and multi-site platforms, 10–100 locations

MSOs, DSOs, veterinary groups, accounting and insurance platforms, franchise systems.
**This is Channel B with money, and it is the best-fitting buyer that exists for this
offer.**

- **Their stated mandate is Isaac's product, in their own words.** A platform's entire
  thesis is *"standardise the operating model across acquired units so integration is
  cheap and EBITDA improves."* That is the engagement, described by the buyer, before
  the first call.
- **They price outcomes at a multiple.** A $500K EBITDA improvement at a 10× exit
  multiple is $5M of enterprise value. **Nobody else Isaac can sell to values his work
  that way** — and a $100K engagement against $5M is not a negotiation.
- Pain extreme (an investment clock), power extreme, targeting easy (portfolio pages are
  public), growing fast, information-dense, and replication is the whole point.

**The risk is real: longer cycle, more than one decision-maker, and adoption risk across
operators.** The export already prescribes the mitigation and it should not be
improvised — `MODEL → OPPORTUNITY MAP → PILOT → EVIDENCE → SYSTEM UPDATE → ROLLOUT`.
**Never open by promising network-wide transformation.** Pilot one location, prove it,
then roll out. And 10–100 units specifically, because the founder or COO is still
reachable and procurement does not yet look like an enterprise.

### 🔴 AVOID

- **Field-service trades with heavy truck-roll** — plumbing, HVAC, landscaping, and
  Alex's own category. Same atoms problem, and it is the mistake of repeating what is
  familiar rather than what scored well.
- **Enterprise.** The export is explicit: the 10–100 band is attractive partly because
  *"procurement is less likely to resemble giant-enterprise bureaucracy."*
- **Under ~$2M revenue.** No budget for $8K, and the honest answer is to say so.
- **Anywhere the buyer is not the operator.** The entire sales mechanism is playing a
  business back to the person who runs it. There is nobody to play it to.
