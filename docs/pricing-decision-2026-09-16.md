## 1. THE REFRAME — "whole" is not a price question, it is a CAPACITY question

The delivery cost of the advisory is fixed and it is **Isaac's hours**: two working
sessions a month plus research, analysis and preparation between them. Call it **10
hours per client per month** — that is the honest number for two sessions plus real
prep, and it does not fall when the price does.

So the only thing price changes is **how many humans Isaac must hold in his head to
reach the same money**:

| price | clients needed for $10K/mo | Isaac's hours/mo on delivery | what is left of his month |
|---|---|---|---|
| **$1,000** | **10** | **100 hrs** | **nothing** |
| **$2,000** | 5 | 50 hrs | half a month |
| **$4,000** | 2.5 → 3 | 30 hrs | most of a month |
| **$8,000** | 1.25 → 2 | 20 hrs | almost all of it |

⇒ **Every halving of price doubles the number of relationships he must maintain for the
same money.** At $1,000 he hits $10K/mo with zero hours left to build the thing the
whole business is downstream of. That is not "getting whole," that is buying a job.

**And his own export already ruled on this:** `Ribcage gets booked → Isaac becomes
bottleneck → raise price → codify the repeated operations`. Discounting does not just
cost margin — **it front-loads the bottleneck and pays less for reaching it.** The
instinct that "maybe that's an amateur move" is correct, and this is the reason.

## 2. THE OSCILLATION IS TWO DIFFERENT NEEDS WEARING ONE PRICE

The four options keep trading off against each other because they are answers to two
questions that got merged:

| the need | what it actually wants | the right instrument |
|---|---|---|
| **cash now** — get whole | 30-day cash, short payback | **payment TERMS** (upfront, prepay) |
| **traces + proof** — find the invariant | more instances, named case studies, testimonials, the right to publish | **an EXCHANGE** (what they give back) |

**Price is the wrong instrument for the second one**, and dropping it to buy proof spends
the anchor permanently to get something the client would have traded for anyway.

**The framework library already says so.** `hormozi-attract-offers`: a discount works for
a **well-understood** service where the buyer knows the going rate (gyms, dentists,
haircuts) and **fails for a complex or novel service the buyer does not understand.** AI
transformation advisory is the second kind — the deck spends five slides establishing
what it is even worth. A discount on a number nobody can price teaches the buyer nothing
except that the number is soft.

⇒ **Do not discount. Change what they give you instead. Add, never subtract.**

## 3. THE RECOMMENDATION

### a. Canonical: **$2,000/month.** Change it now, not later.

Not because $2,000 is the right number in some absolute sense — because **at
month-to-month with no lock-in, price is the cheapest variable in the business to
change.** Either party can end it with written notice; nothing is owed. So the cost of
picking wrong is *one conversation*, and the cost of not picking is *no pipeline*. The
"double it until you start smiling" test is real, and this is the cheapest business in
the world to run it in. $2,000 is one doubling. Run it on the next three calls.

⚠ The thing that makes this safe is the thing already in the signed agreement. Do not
add a term, ever — the no-lock-in clause is what makes repricing free.

### b. The first three: **Founding Partners at $2,000, with an exchange — not a cut.**

They pay the rate card. They get things that cost zero marginal dollars today:

- **their rate locked for as long as they stay** — costs nothing now, worth a lot later,
  and it gets more valuable every time the price goes up (which is the plan)
- **first call on implementation capacity** — pure scheduling, free to give
- **named as a founding partner** in the published method

Isaac gets the thing he actually needs and cannot buy any other way:

- **a named case study and a testimonial**, agreed in writing up front
- **the right to publish the map and the method** produced in their engagement
- **one warm introduction** to a comparable operator

⇒ This is the export's own epistemology executed correctly: he needs *traces* to find
what is Alex-specific and what is invariant. Traces are bought with exchange, not with
price.

### c. For cash now, move the TERMS, not the rate.

| structure | 30-day cash | effective monthly | what the rate card still says |
|---|---|---|---|
| the export's cohort proposal — $3,000 up front / 90 days | $3,000 | **$1,000** ⚠ | a 50% cut, delivered as a lump |
| **recommended — 3 months prepaid at $2,000, 4th month free** | **$6,000** | $1,500 over 4 | **$2,000** ✅ |
| plain month-to-month | $2,000 | $2,000 | $2,000 |

The middle row is the same generosity as the export's proposal, shaped as a **bonus**
instead of a **discount**: 2× the 30-day cash, the anchor untouched, and a free month is
a far better thing to say on a call than a lower number. (`hormozi-cfa`: 30-day cash is
the lever that decides whether acquisition can be self-financed at all.)

### d. Alex stays at $1,000 — and gets *upgraded*, not raised.

He is the only trace. Raising him is the fastest way to lose the thing that makes every
other page on the site true. Instead: **grandfather him at $1,000 permanently and call it
Founding Partner #1** — a gift that costs exactly nothing, because $1,000 is already the
number he pays — **in exchange for the named case study and the testimonial.**

That converts the number Isaac feels weird about into the single most valuable asset the
business does not yet have.

|---|---|---|---|
| $4,000 | 2 clients | 20 | this quarter, easily |
| $6,000 | 3 clients | 30 | the founding three, exactly |

| $20,000 | 10 clients | 100 | not without delegation |

**The founding-three plan lands on $6,000/mo, and prepaid it is $18,000 of 30-day cash.**
If "whole" is at or under $6,000, the plan in §3 gets there with three phone calls and
Isaac keeps most of his month. If it is materially above that, the answer is not a
higher close rate — it is a higher price or a second person, and that is a different
conversation.
