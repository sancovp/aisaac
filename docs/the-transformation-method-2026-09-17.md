## 1. THE METHOD — VERBATIM (Isaac, 2026-09-17)

> What i do is i map out:
>
> For every department in your business, what are its roles and processes?
> For each role is there a JD, for each JD does it include SOPs for each of these processes
> so the JD is the closure of the SOPs for the role?
> For this role, which processes:
>
> * have automatable steps
> * have services that help
> * have services that can automate them
> * can become agents
> * could be custom agents/teams/networks
> * what would that likely look like (quick design sketch)
>
> And
>
> Which ones are related to revenue?
> Which ones make the most revenue?
> Which ones hurt the most/take the most time?
> Which ones dont even make sense?
> What are the levers to them (params)?
> How do we turn this all into data?
>
> What culture changes are required (through the SOPs and JDs/manuals, and reward
> structures, etc) to incentivize employees to use the AI system they are automating
> themselves out of jobs with?
>
> Then, for your entire business we map out:
> Given all this, what does a:
>
> * DTO look like
> * Do you have any potentially productizable automations *before* you reach the point of
>   business model in a box?
>
> Then we go:
>
> So what kind of business do you want to transform into?

**DTO = Digital Twin of Organization** (Isaac, same exchange, in answer to the agent's
refusal to guess the term).

## 2. THE READING — five layers and a question

Subordinate to §1. Written so a surface author can tell what each layer is *for*.

| # | layer | what it produces |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **the org as a formal structure** | department → roles → processes → SOPs, with **the JD as the CLOSURE of the role's SOPs** |
| 2 | **the automation ladder, per process** | the six-way classification, up to custom agents/teams/networks, plus a design sketch |
| 3 | **the economics overlay** | revenue attribution, pain/time, the processes that *don't make sense*, the **levers as params**, and the whole thing turned into DATA |
| 4 | **the culture layer** | the incentive design that makes employees adopt the system that automates them out of their jobs — implemented in the SAME artifacts (SOPs, JDs, manuals, reward structures) |
| 5 | **the whole business** | the **DTO** (Digital Twin of Organization), and the productizable automations found *before* the business-model-in-a-box endpoint |
| → | **the question** | *"So what kind of business do you want to transform into?"* |

### What each layer carries that the deck's surface does not

- **JD = closure of SOPs is a TEST, not a description.** A role is complete exactly when its
  JD carries an SOP for every process the role runs. So "it's not documented" (deck obstacle
  4) stops being a complaint and becomes a computable hole with a named location. The gaps
  are the finding.
- **The ladder is a ladder.** Each process is classified by the highest rung it can reach —
  automatable steps → a service that helps → a service that automates → an agent → a network
  of custom agents. Same shape as every other ladder in this ecosystem: take the highest
  rung the thing can be made in, and say why the rung above cannot make it.
- **"Levers (params)" is the hinge of the whole method.** Naming a process's levers turns it
  from a story into a parameterized function. Params plus data is what makes layer 5
  possible at all — a twin you can *run*.
- **"Which ones don't even make sense?"** — the only question in the set whose answer is
  DELETE rather than automate. It is also the one that proves the vendor-neutrality the deck
  claims, because it costs the seller money to ask it.
- **The culture layer is the honest one.** Everybody selling AI transformation steps around
  "your staff will realize they are building their own replacement." Naming it, and solving
  it in the same objects as the automation, is the trust move of the entire pitch.
- **The close is a TRANSFORMATION QUESTION, not a savings claim.** The engagement does not
  end at "you saved twelve hours a week." It ends with the owner holding a complete,
  parameterized, data-form model of their own company — including the parts that make no
  sense — and being asked what they want it to become.

### The through-line to the frozen surfaces

This is the same arc the deck's slide 5 draws (FOUNDATION → HARDENING → CLOSURE, seven
stages, ending in a governed AI operating system whose ownership is **client-owned ·
licensed · hybrid**) — read from the inside. "Licensed" is the business-model-in-a-box
endpoint, and it is the same object the franchisor play sells upward
([`franchisor-play-2026-09-16.md`](franchisor-play-2026-09-16.md)): one engagement's
productized automations become the thing a franchisor multiplies across its franchisees.
