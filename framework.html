The test

Do you know how you make money?

How to know if you know

Is everything every role does learned through a JD that is in a manual made of SOPs for everything they do?

If it isn’t, your business culture is made of other stuff — and that other stuff is why you aren’t making more money. A process nobody has written down can’t be followed, can’t be measured, and can’t be handed to AI.

The two shapes of a business that fails it

1You pay for processes that are people’s supposed skills, and money falls out.

2You’re still doing everything yourself, even though you have a team.

Both are equally not a real business yet. The framework is how you find out which one you are — and what to write down first.

What’s inside

Seven parts. Work through them for your own business.

1

The org map

For every department in your business: what are its roles, and what are their processes?

2

The closure test

For each role, is there a JD — and does it include an SOP for every process that role runs? A role is complete when its JD is made of its SOPs. The gaps are your first finding.

3

The automation ladder

For every process: does it have automatable steps? Services that help? Services that can automate it? Could it become an agent — or a custom team of agents? And a quick sketch of what that would look like.

4

The money questions

Which processes are related to revenue? Which make the most? Which hurt the most or take the most time? Which don’t even make sense? What are their levers? How do you turn it all into data?

5

The culture question

What has to change in your SOPs, JDs and reward structures so your people actually use the AI system that takes over their busywork?

6

The whole business

Given all of that: what would a digital twin of your organization look like — and are there automations in there you could turn into products?

7

The question

So what kind of business do you want to transform into?

The autonomy ladder: how a business hands work to AI without losing control.

The workers

The people and agents that do the work — each running written procedures.

Management

An AI that watches the work, checks it against what “done” means, and adjusts how the workers are set up.

You

You set the goals and say yes or no. You govern the system — you don’t manage it.

  1. Grade 1In the loopYou approve each piece of work before it runs, and every yes or no you give tunes the system.
  2. Grade 2On the loopThe parts that have proven themselves become routines. You review them after the fact instead of before.
  3. Grade 3At the autonomy you choseThe system runs as independently as you decided it should — and the way it got there becomes a pattern you can reuse.
  4. Grade 4It climbs the ladder itself — or climbs a new oneEither it takes over running its own promotions, or it extends into a new part of the business.

Trust is earned per area and never transfers. Anything new starts again at grade 1, however well the rest of the system runs.

Build an AIOS

An AIOS is a folder an AI reads into — and becomes.

Point an agent at the folder and it takes on the role, the rules and the skills inside it. That is the whole trick: the operating system is written down, so any agent can run it.

  1. CLAUDE.mdWho you are hereThe role an agent takes on the moment it reads into the folder.
  2. rules/The loop and the lawsHow work runs here, what is never allowed, the safety lines — always loaded.
  3. skills/The tree of howHow to think, how to decide, and the workflow — one root, the rest reached by walking down, never a flat pile.
  4. kb/What it learnedThe record of what worked, so the next run starts ahead.
  5. toolsCode, only where it must runMath, files, APIs, anything with a real effect. Everything else is an instruction.

A thing is code only if it must execute. The numbers are computed by tools; how to think lives in attention chains; decisions live in chains of reasoning; the pipeline is a workflow the agent walks. Gated, addressed by coordinate, and it grows by adding to its own tree.

The levels

ChainCompiler: every level of it, one type all the way up.

Everything reduces to one thing — a skill folder, the unit any agent loads. Every level makes or uses only that, so the levels stack and recurse.

  1. The gaterulecatcherLearns a notation’s grammar from examples, then refuses text that breaks it — so the model never drifts out of its own syntax.
  2. ACAttention chainsA silent template for how to think: [Symptom] ⇒ [Repro] ⇒ [Hypothesis] ⇒ |Localize|.
  3. CoRChains of reasoningThe same chain spoken as a paragraph that makes a decision.
  4. SCSkill chainsAttention chains, chains of reasoning and ordinary skills in sequence, rolled into one skill — and a skill chain can chain skill chains.
  5. SkillTreeThe organizationA tree the agent walks one layer at a time, so nothing it doesn’t need fills its head.
  6. GBAThe bandit agentAn AIOS that decides, task by task, whether to reuse a proven chain or build a new one — and keeps what works.
  7. COGChallenger · Observer · GeneratorThree bandit agents: one builds, one checks, one watches the strategy.
  8. HBAThe hierarchical agentSeats that hand the heavy work to their own subagents, so no single window has to hold it all.
