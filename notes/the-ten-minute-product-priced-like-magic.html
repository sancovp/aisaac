The $5,000 Product That Was Ten Minutes of Work

I Paid an AI Agency $5,000. Then I Found Out What They Actually Did. Strip the branding off a typical "AI agent" and the setup is about ten minutes of work.

The anecdote

Strip the branding off a typical "AI agent" sold by an agency and you find: a system prompt someone wrote in an afternoon, a few tools wired to a calendar and a texting service, deployed on a platform they're reselling at a markup, fronted by a dashboard with the client's logo on it. The setup is about ten minutes of actual work. The price is five figures. The price reflects the brand, not the build.

This isn't a conspiracy, and it isn't new. Web agencies charged five figures for template sites. Social media managers charged thousands a month to use a scheduling tool. SEO shops charged for keyword tools that cost a hundred bucks. The markup is always rent on a knowledge gap — the distance between "I know this exists and how to combine it" and "you don't." What AI changed isn't the pattern; it's that the gap is now trivially closeable. Anyone with a weekend and curiosity can close it. So the tasks whole industries are built on have quietly collapsed to minutes — and the only question left that matters is what are you actually building?

The honest move that falls out of seeing this is counterintuitive: give the map away. Lay out the whole board for free. Because if you genuinely get it, you don't need to buy anything from anyone — including from the person who drew the map. And if you get it and want to go faster, that's what a guide is for. The map being free is what makes it trustworthy; the gap it reveals is real, not manufactured.

What it reveals (the shape of the deeper rung)

  • There's a ladder, and most people (and most buyers) can't see which rung they're on. The lower rungs are commoditized; the markup there is rent on a closing gap. The durable value lives higher, where the thing can't be replicated by watching a tutorial.
  • The trustworthy position is honesty about altitude — telling people which rung they actually need, which is often a low one they should simply stop at, rather than selling everyone the high-rung story.
  • The real, non-fakeable distance is between "configuring" and "engineering." The gap that can't be closed with a weekend is the upper rungs — and that gap is exactly the difference between being able to see a capable system and being able to build one.

What you can take from it right now

This is the anti-scam tool, and it's free: for anything you're paying for, ask whether the price is on the work or on a knowledge gap you could close yourself — and if it's the gap, you have two cheaper options (close it, or jump it deliberately). Seeing the board is the whole gift. Plenty of people will read this, realize they don't need to buy anything, and that's the honest outcome.

Honest caveat. What this doesn't cover: the path up the ladder. The map is free on purpose — if you genuinely get it, you don't need to buy anything from anyone, including from me.
