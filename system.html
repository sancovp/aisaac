What the engine is made of

Five layers. Every world runs on the same five.

Point it at a different domain and the departments change. The stack underneath does not — which is the whole reason one engine can carry a plumbing company, a coding team and a body.

The runtime

The open agent runtime everything else is built on

Free, installable, and inspectable on GitHub. Given away on purpose — the map is not the moat.

Memory

Agents act from a knowledge graph, not a flat prompt

What a world learns accumulates in a graph instead of resetting each session. This is the mechanism behind the word “compounds.”

Self-building

Departments compile themselves from a written spec

You describe a department; the engine writes it and hires it. That is how a world grows its own team instead of you assembling one.

The logic layer

It checks the work against your rules before it acts

Written rules, checked at every hand-off — not a vibe check by the same model that produced the work. The reason the output holds up.

Rounds

The world runs in rounds and grades its own last one

Each round reviews what the previous round claimed was finished, keeps what worked, and adjusts the next batch. A simulation, not a script.

What to expect

Not a better answer today. A system that still knows it next month.

The usual pitch in this category will not survive contact, so here is the version that does: on any single task, a well-written prompt matches this. One question at a time, side by side, you would not reliably pick the engine out of a line-up. Anyone claiming otherwise is selling you the demo.

What changes is the second step, and the tenth. A one-off answer is excellent and then it is gone. What is described above remembers, recalls what it already decided, commits to a structure up front instead of re-deciding it every turn, and extends itself when a job runs past what it knows. That difference does not show in a snapshot and is decisive over a month — which is an awkward thing to sell, and the honest thing to say.

So: nothing here is instant. Expect fast, coherent and self-operating, and expect the compounding to show over weeks rather than in the first sitting. Expect a person in the loop too — a human reviews the batch before it goes out, on purpose, every time.
